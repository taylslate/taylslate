## Objective and positioning

The session describes a pre-customer company whose immediate objective is one real transaction through Taylslate. Market demand remains unproven. Two complementary positioning angles are recorded:

- Execute a brand's deliberately chosen deal, sourced anywhere, including direct relationships and shows outside a marketplace catalog.
- Offer transparent economics against the manual, opaque-markup agency workflow.

The open market question is how many brands buy chosen, direct sponsorships and feel enough operational pain to change their workflow. The session argues this requires conversations and transactions, not further abstract reasoning.

The data advantage is prospective: real prices, repeat buying, delivery, and settlement outcomes accumulated by facilitating transactions. Payments alone are not the differentiated asset; the system of record and its transaction data are the thesis. No established moat or measured performance advantage is demonstrated by this note.

## Initial validation buyer

Mid-size DTC brands that deliberately choose shows and buy host-read ads, have a reachable founder or growth lead making media decisions, and buy often enough for manual work to hurt.

Qualification questions: Do they repeat shows? Is the decision-maker reachable? Is the pain sufficient to consider switching?

This supplies the historical answer to the first-buyer ambiguity: **the July validation effort targeted existing direct buyers**, while older company documents described the broader opportunity as brands new to the channel. ## Validation evidence and sales approach

The proposed signal ladder increases in strength from stated interest, to demonstrated workflow pain, to a meaningful investment of time or reputation, to a campaign commitment with budget/date, to a transaction and repeat transaction. The original's “10×” comparison is rhetorical, not a measured probability or scoring model.

Suggested conversation: understand current buying pain, demonstrate the relevant workflow, and ask for a small real campaign. The proposed initial offer is one campaign or one show, founder-assisted, with pay-as-delivered terms. Statements suggesting the buyer has no exposure are sales framing in the source, not verified risk-free guarantees.

Warm introductions are preferred. A small prospect list can be sourced from sponsor mentions and promo codes in recent show notes. These notes recommend ten relevant brands and three actual conversations rather than a large automated list. No completed outreach or outcomes are reported.
